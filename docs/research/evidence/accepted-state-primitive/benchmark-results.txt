INTERPRETATION
--------------

1. HOT-PATH READ ALLOCATION — the load-bearing E3 result.

   readAcceptedRatio                gc.alloc.rate.norm = ≈ 10⁻⁶ B/op
   readAcceptedRouteRatioCount      gc.alloc.rate.norm = ≈ 10⁻⁶ B/op
   readAcceptedSequenceAndPolicy    gc.alloc.rate.norm = ≈ 10⁻⁶ B/op

   JMH prints "≈ 10⁻⁶" when total allocation across the whole run is a handful
   of bytes attributable to harness infrastructure rather than to the measured
   operation. gc.count is ≈ 0 for all three: no collection was triggered.

   This is corroborated by an independent EXACT measurement in
   S01HotPathAllocationTest, which uses the HotSpot per-thread allocation
   counter and observes exactly 0 bytes over 2 000 000 reads. The two methods
   agree, and the exact counter is what the E3 zero-allocation claim rests on;
   the JMH figure is the statistical confirmation.

   Reading a nested reference field (routeRatios) costs no allocation either,
   confirming there is no copy-on-read anywhere on the path.

2. HOT-PATH READ LATENCY — indicative, not a budget.

   0.890 / 1.040 / 1.193 ns/op for one, two and three field reads behind a
   single volatile reference load. The progression is consistent with the read
   being exactly what it claims: one AtomicReference.get() followed by plain
   final-field reads, with no lock and no wrapper construction.

3. READS UNDER A COMMIT STORM.

   contendedRead   8.146 ns/op with 7 reader threads while a writer commits
                   continuously. Readers are never blocked, because they take no
                   lock and observe only whole immutable envelopes.
   contendedCommit 266.722 ns/op for one writer.

   The commit figure INCLUDES construction of a fresh candidate policy (a record
   plus a one-entry immutable Map) on every call. It is therefore an upper bound
   on candidate construction plus one compareAndSet, not the cost of the store
   itself. The commit rate driven here is orders of magnitude above real
   operation, where policy changes arrive a few times per minute; this is an
   intentional stress mode.

4. WHY THE GROUP FIGURE IS 1.021 B/op AND NOT ZERO.

   readWhileCommit:·gc.alloc.rate.norm = 1.021 B/op is the GROUP AGGREGATE. It
   amortises the writer thread's two allocations per commit (one candidate
   policy plus one accepted envelope) across the operations of all eight threads
   in the group. It is NOT reader allocation.

   The reader-only benchmarks in section 1 are the hot-path evidence. The
   per-commit allocation counted here is cold-path allocation, which the E3
   contract permits: a new immutable envelope per accepted commit is exactly how
   the design achieves a zero-allocation read.

5. WHAT IS NOT MEASURED HERE.

   No comparative benchmark against VersionedStateHolder's retry loop was run.
   The retry-amplification difference is established behaviourally instead, in
   S01VersionedStateHolderContrastTest, which shows builder re-invocation and
   rebase deterministically rather than statistically. Note for P5: because
   SamplerState's constructor calls SamplingPolicySnapshotFactory.create(),
   every retry of the current primitive re-normalises and re-compiles a policy
   snapshot, so retry amplification there is allocation-heavy, not merely a
   repeated CAS. Quantifying that is P5 scope, not P0.

The corrected payload adds no work to the read: the eight Category A leaves are
seven primitives/enum plus one already-materialised unmodifiable map reference,
and no defensive copy, normalisation or validation happens on the read path.
Defensive copying and normalisation happen once per candidate construction, which
is cold-path.

WHAT WOULD REQUIRE A NEW BENCHMARK
----------------------------------
A new measurement becomes necessary if any of the following changes:

  - the read returns anything other than a published immutable reference;
  - the route-ratio map is materialised, copied or sorted during a read;
  - the store acquires a lock, a retry loop or a second atomic;
  - the payload gains a field that a hot-path reader must compute.

None of these is true of the PROPOSED contract fixed by this correction.
